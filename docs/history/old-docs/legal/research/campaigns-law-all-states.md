# Campaigns: all-state audit of the law and the charges (Feature 6)

**Status:** research, 2026-11-11. Every one of the 50 states and DC has a row. **This is not legal advice.** It was put together from about twenty law-firm, accounting-firm and compliance-vendor pages (listed at the end), read in full where they could be opened. **No statute was read.** The sources disagree with each other often; where they do the cell says so instead of picking one. A cell that says "none found" means none of the pages read mentioned a rule, **not** that the state has none.

How sure each kind of cell is:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| plain text | Two or more sources agree |
| *(1)* | One source only |
| **?** | Sources disagree; both versions are given |
| none found | No page read covered it |

Companion: [`campaigns-law-by-state.md`](campaigns-law-by-state.md) (federal layer, build rules, lawyer questions). Where the two differ, this file is the newer one.

## 1. Federal charges (apply in every state)

| Law | Charge |
| --- | --- |
| TCPA, private suit | **$500 per call or text, up to $1,500 if willful or knowing.** No cap, no proof of harm, class actions. |
| Telemarketing Sales Rule (FTC) | Civil penalty about **$51,744 per call** (one source says $43,792: the figure is raised yearly). |
| CAN-SPAM (email), FTC | Up to about **$53,088 per email**. No private suit for individuals. |
| National Do Not Call registry | Subscription paid by the caller per area code (price set yearly by the FTC: *verify*); list must be no older than 31 days. |
| 10DLC texting registration (carriers) | Brand and campaign fees charged by the texting provider; not a law, but texts do not deliver without it. |

### What the table adds up to

- **Text-specific state laws found in 14 states:** Arizona, Connecticut, Florida, Maryland, Missouri, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia, Washington, and New Jersey's cell-phone sales-call ban. In the rest the federal rules and the state's general telemarketing and unfair-practice law apply.
- **Registration or a licence** is reported for about 40 states and DC. Known charges run from **$0 to $1,500 in fees** and **$10,000 to $100,000 in bonds**. If a customer texted or called into every state that charges, the fees listed in the table add up to roughly **$5,000 to $7,000 a year** and the bonds to roughly **$480,000 to $620,000 in face value** (16 to 19 states, depending on the disputed rows; most states' fees were not found, so the real totals are higher) (a surety bond usually costs 1 to 5 percent of its face value a year: *estimate, verify*). Most states exempt some sellers; whether real-estate investors are exempt is not in any source.
- **Quiet hours:** the strictest states are Rhode Island (weekdays 9 to 6, Saturday 10 to 5, no Sundays or holidays), Kentucky (not before 10 am), Pennsylvania (9 to 7, no Sundays), Maine for automated calls (weekdays 9 to 5) and Alabama, Louisiana and Mississippi (no Sundays).
- **Frequency:** Florida, Maryland, Oklahoma and Oregon cap contacts at 3 per 24 hours; Maine caps automated calls at one per 8 hours.

## 3. Email, state by state

The detail here is thin: the only complete list found (Cornell's) is old (it cites nothing after 2009) and gives no detail, and two of the better pages could not be opened.

- **States with a commercial-email statute per Cornell's list (32 and DC):** Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, DC, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Missouri, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wyoming. Utah is described elsewhere as having a newer act with a private right of action.
- **Not on that list (not verified either way):** Alabama, Alaska, Hawaii, Kentucky, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Mississippi, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, South Carolina, South Dakota, Utah, Vermont, Wisconsin.
- Federal CAN-SPAM overrides state email rules **except** those about falsity or deception. Those are where the charges are:

| State | What is punished | Charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| California (B&P §17529.5) | Misleading subject lines, false headers, third-party domains used without permission | **$1,000 per email**, capped (about $1,000,000 per incident: *verify*), or actual damages; private suits rising |
| Washington (CEMA, RCW 19.190) | False or misleading subject lines, disguised origin | Plaintiffs claim **$500 per email**; dozens of class actions in 2025 |
| Maryland (MCEMA) | False or misleading email | Private suits; about ten filed in one recent window |
| Florida | Misleading subject lines | Class actions began in 2026 |
| Utah, Virginia, Indiana | Misleading commercial email | Private rights reported; amounts not found |

Practical rule, unchanged: the subject line must be true, the header must be the customer's own domain, and every email carries the postal address and a working unsubscribe.

## 5. What changes in the build rules

These replace rule 4 and add to rules 3 and 5 in [`campaigns-law-by-state.md`](campaigns-law-by-state.md) section 5.

1. **Quiet hours come from a per-state table, not one window.** No single window was right: 9 am to 7 pm breaks Rhode Island (ends 6 pm, Saturday 10 to 5) and Kentucky (starts 10 am). For any state with no confirmed row, and until a lawyer confirms the table, the fallback is **Monday to Friday, 10 am to 5 pm local time, no weekends, no federal or state holidays**: that is inside every window in section 2.
2. **Sundays and holidays are off by default everywhere**, and a holiday calendar per state is needed (Louisiana includes Mardi Gras and Good Friday; Alabama lists 17).
3. **Emergency holds:** New York and Louisiana ban solicitation into declared emergency areas: a per-state, per-county pause switch.
4. **Frequency cap stays 3 per 24 hours per lead**, across calls and texts, with a lower campaign default; automated voice in Maine is one per 8 hours.
5. **State gate:** a per-state record on the workspace: registration or licence number, bond, expiry. A lead in a state that needs one, with nothing recorded, is held. The gate also holds wholesaling messages for Pennsylvania, Illinois, Virginia, Iowa, Nebraska, New Mexico, South Carolina and Kentucky unless the workspace has recorded a licence.
6. **Wholesaling disclosures** are per-state templates the customer must turn on (Ohio's bold notice, Oklahoma's boxed notice and so on belong in the contract, not the text; the message must not contradict them and must not claim to be the buyer of record when the plan is to assign).
7. **Opt-outs are kept 10 years** (Virginia) and applied across every campaign of the workspace on that channel.
8. **Do-not-call scrubbing** covers the federal registry and each state list in section 2 (Colorado, Florida, Indiana, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Missouri, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Texas and the disputed ones).
9. **New Jersey:** no marketing calls or texts to cell phones without a prior relationship or consent, whatever the hours.
10. **Charges are shown to the customer before a campaign starts:** the states in the campaign, which need registration, and the fee and bond listed here, marked as unverified.

## 6. What is still not known (a lawyer or the statutes must settle these)

- Every cell marked **?** or *(1)*, and every "none found".
- Whether registration and bonds fall on the platform, the customer, or both; and which exemptions cover a real-estate investor contacting an owner about buying (many state laws define a "telephone solicitation" as selling goods or services: an offer to **buy** a house may fall outside some of them, which no source addressed).
- Email statutes for the 49 states without detail.
- City rules (Philadelphia's do-not-solicit registry is the only one found).
- The current federal Do Not Call subscription price and the bond premium rates.
