# Acceptable Use and Calling Policy

Version 2026-11-10. This policy is part of the [Terms of Service](/terms). You are responsible for every call, text and message you make or send with Caller Whale. Caller Whale gives you tools; it does not give legal advice and does not check that a call is lawful.

## 1. Before you call or text

1. **Know who you are allowed to contact.** Use lists you have the right to use. For marketing calls and texts to a person's mobile number you generally need that person's prior express written consent; for automated or prerecorded calls and texts the rules are stricter.
2. **Check the Do Not Call lists.** Scrub numbers against the National Do Not Call Registry and the state lists that apply, and keep your own internal do-not-call list. Honour a request to stop at once and record it. Mark the lead **Do not call** in Caller Whale and keep that flag.
3. **Respect calling hours.** Calls to a person's number may generally not be made before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the person's local time, and some states are stricter. Use the lead's time zone.
4. **Identify yourself.** Give your name, the business and a contact number, and show a true caller ID. Do not spoof numbers. Follow the rules on call labelling and caller authentication.
5. **Recording.** Some states require everyone on a call to agree to recording. Do not record unless you have what the law requires.
6. **Texts.** Follow carrier and CTIA rules and the opt-out words (STOP). Stop on request.
7. **Email.** Follow CAN-SPAM: true sender, working opt-out, no deceptive subject lines.

## 2. Data you must not upload

Without a lawful basis agreed with us in writing: health records, bank or card numbers, government ID numbers, driver or motor-vehicle records, data about children under 18, biometric data, or any data collected by deceit or against a site's terms. Lists with consumer-report data (credit, tenant or employment screening) may be used only as that law permits.

## 3. What you must not do with Caller Whale

Do not use it to harass, threaten, defraud or mislead; to call or text people who asked you to stop; to hide who you are; to scrape or resell data; to decide a person's credit, insurance, job or housing; to send malware; or to test or attack the service without written permission.

## 4. What Caller Whale does to help

Caller Whale stores a do-not-call flag on every lead and a time zone for follow-ups, so your team can see both. Applying them before each call remains your responsibility. Administrators can switch off the learning copy and erase it. People may ask to be removed from the learning copy at [Remove my information](/remove-my-information). We may suspend a workspace that breaks this policy, and we will tell you why when we can.
